Common Water Damage Issues in San Jose

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for San Jose households. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in San Jose. Clean water from the tank or supply is one thing, but water from the bowl after use is contaminated. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.
