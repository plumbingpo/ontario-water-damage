No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at Ontario Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Ontario

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.
