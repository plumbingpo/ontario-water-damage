Why Flood Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.

Maintenance Tips for Flood Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Clear leaves and debris from storm drains and gutters, which lets water drain the way it should
  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • Add downspout extensions so roof water empties well away from the foundation
  • A battery-powered backup sump pump keeps water moving if the electricity fails in a storm

Signs It Is Time for Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Soft, wet drywall or carpeting that is soaked through
  • Mud, silt or debris left on floors once the water goes down
  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • Storm damage that lets rain in through the roof or walls
  • Water collecting along the foundation because the yard is saturated
  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
