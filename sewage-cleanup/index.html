A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in Ontario, Ontario Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
